Reading the result honestly

A day total is a question, not a verdict.

Inside the range

Inside the published range does not mean everything is fine, and outside it does not mean anything is wrong. A single day cannot show a pattern, and individual sleep needs vary.

Outside the range

The checker says “outside the published range — worth raising with your care team”. It never says too little or too much, because it cannot know that.

Under 4 months

No published range exists, so there is nothing to compare against. The checker reports the total and the wide normal spread instead of scoring it.

What a total cannot see

Weight gain, feeding, diaper output, breathing, and how the baby behaves awake are the things a clinician looks at. None of them are on this page, which is why it routes concerns rather than answering them.

Who slept is missing

A total says nothing about which caregiver covered which hours. That is why the night shift planner exists, and why Dad Recovery is a feature rather than an afterthought.

When to call instead

Poor feeding, fewer wet diapers, unusual breathing, fever, or anything that feels wrong is a call to your pediatrician or emergency services — not a spreadsheet.

Safety note: these tools do arithmetic on the times you enter, and they organize what you already know. They do not diagnose, do not assess development, do not recommend sleep training or any treatment, and cannot tell you why a baby is waking. The published safe-sleep rules do not change because a week went badly: place babies on their back for every sleep until 1 year old, keep soft bedding and sitting devices out of the sleep space, stop swaddling once a baby starts rolling, and remember that no product can prevent SIDS. Do not delay professional or emergency care to use a tool. Individual sleep needs vary — follow the child's qualified care team.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

How many hours should my baby sleep in 24 hours?

For babies 4 to 12 months the published AASM recommendation, endorsed by the AAP, is 12 to 16 hours in a 24-hour period including naps. For 1 to 2 years it is 11 to 14 hours, and for 3 to 5 years it is 10 to 13 hours. For babies under 4 months the AASM does not publish a recommendation, because the data is limited and newborn sleep varies so widely; AAP HealthyChildren describes newborns as sleeping about 16 to 17 hours a day in 1- to 2-hour stretches, and Sleep Foundation puts the normal spread anywhere from 11 to 19 hours. This page's checker adds up the hours you enter and puts the total next to those published numbers. It does not decide whether your baby is sleeping enough.

Does the 24-hour sleep total checker diagnose anything?

No, and it is built not to. It adds up the night stretches and naps you type in and reports the total, how it splits between night and daytime sleep, and whether the total sits inside the published range for the age band you selected. A total outside that range is reported as a question for your care team, never as “too little” or “too much”, because a single day's total cannot tell you whether a baby is well. It does not assess development, does not recommend sleep training, and cannot tell you why a baby is waking. Individual sleep needs vary, and your pediatrician is the person who can interpret your baby's pattern.

Does anything I type get saved or uploaded?

No. The checker runs entirely in your browser tab. Nothing is saved, submitted, or uploaded, there is no account, and refreshing or closing the tab clears every entry. That is deliberate: a record of when your household was awake and who was holding the baby is not something that should leave the house by accident. To keep a permanent record, print the sheet or log it in DadYolked.

Which sleep tools should I start with?

Start with the question you are actually holding. Sleep tracker for dads if you do not yet have a record. Wake window calculator if you need the next nap window. This checker if you want to know whether the hours add up. 4-month sleep regression for dads if the wakings have become the problem. Night shift schedule if the issue is who is awake. Newborn schedule if you want the whole day in one shape. None of them replace your pediatrician.
