What to track

A shared baby tracker should be selective.

Handoff facts first

Caregivers need feeds, diapers, sleep, medicine, symptoms, and notes that affect the next shift.

Privacy still matters

Baby records can reveal health, routines, locations, and family stress. Sharing should be intentional.

Exports beat chaos

When a pediatrician or caregiver needs context, a clear summary is better than screenshots scattered across chat threads.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

What should a shared baby tracker include?

Feeds, diapers, sleep, medicine, symptoms, milestones, caregiver notes, and exportable summaries.

Does every baby record need to be shared?

No. Share what helps care. Keep sensitive family and health-adjacent notes private when they do not need to travel.

Is DadYolked account-free?

DadYolked is privacy-first with no account required for core tracking.

Does this printable handoff sheet save or upload baby information?

No. The blank handoff sheet runs in the browser and does not save, submit, or upload what you write on a printed copy. Handoff notes can still contain private family health information, so store and share them carefully and only through channels your care team accepts.
